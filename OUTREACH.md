# Post-Application Outreach

Use outreach as a measured second phase after a verified application batch. It should create relevant professional conversations, not a bulk-email campaign.

## Trigger

Set a clear daily application target in the private system. Start outreach only after the tracker verifies that target using exact Application IDs, dates, and `Applied` statuses. Do not infer completion from browser history, folders, or conversational counts.

If the user explicitly requests outreach for a partial day, filter the private tracker by the exact `Date Applied` and `Applied` status, report the actual application count, and prepare only those rows. Never pad the batch to the nominal daily target.

For historical applications, run a separate pilot ordered by recency, fit, and priority. Do not contact hundreds of employers at once.

## Choose one person per company

Research up to two candidates so a fallback exists, but send one initial message:

1. Hiring manager or functional leader within one or two levels of the role.
2. Recruiter or talent partner aligned to the function, location, or early-career program.
3. Closely related employee, alum, or shared connection who can offer informed advice.
4. Founder or executive only when the company is small and the person plausibly participates in hiring.

Use public company pages, alumni context, manual LinkedIn research, and reputable business-contact tools. Prefer a verified work address. Do not use private personal addresses or imply a relationship that does not exist.

LinkedIn prohibits third-party software that scrapes or automates activity on its website. Keep LinkedIn research, invitations, and messages manual.

## Write a short, specific note

The first message should usually be 70-120 words and contain:

- a clear subject naming the role or shared context;
- the exact role already applied to;
- why the person is relevant;
- one evidence-backed connection between the role and the candidate's experience;
- one small ask, such as a brief perspective or 10-15 minute conversation;
- a concise signature and professional link.

Do not ask a stranger directly for a job or referral. Do not attach a resume in the first note. Avoid generic praise, inflated familiarity, and mass-mail wording.

### Hiring manager or team lead

Subject: `Applied for [Role] - question about [team or problem]`

Hi [First name],

I applied for the [Role] position and wanted to reach out because [specific reason tied to their team or work]. In my current role, I [one supported result or responsibility]. That overlap is what drew me to [specific responsibility, product, or problem in the posting].

If you have 10-15 minutes, I would value your perspective on [one thoughtful question]. No worries if timing is tight, and thank you for reading.

Best,
[Name]

### Recruiter

Subject: `Application for [Role] - [one relevant strength]`

Hi [First name],

I applied for [Role] today. The position closely matches my experience with [two or three supported capabilities], including [one concrete result or scope]. I am especially interested in [specific team, mission, or responsibility].

If helpful, I would be glad to share additional context. I would also appreciate any perspective on what the team values most in this hire.

Best,
[Name]

### Alum or near-peer

Subject: `[Shared context] - quick question about [Company or field]`

Hi [First name],

I came across your background while researching [Company] after applying for [Role]. I noticed [real shared context or specific career path]. I am currently [brief, accurate professional context].

Would you be open to a brief 10-15 minute conversation about your experience at [Company] and what helped you succeed on the team? I would value your perspective.

Best,
[Name]

## Draft, confirm, send, and track

Before sending, create one private Outreach row per contact and link it to the exact Application ID. Record the profile source, work email, verification method, reason for selection, subject, and complete draft.

Use `Draft ready` only when the work email is verified and the note has passed review. Keep invalid, unverified, unavailable, or still-enriching addresses in `Researching` so they cannot enter the send queue accidentally.

Audit names, role, company, claims, links, and destination address. Prepare the entire batch, then obtain any required action-time confirmation immediately before sending. Record a message as Sent only after the interface visibly confirms the send.

Never publish the live outreach table. It contains personal data and private correspondence.

## Follow up once

If there is no reply, follow up once in the same thread after five business days. Keep it to about 40-70 words and add a useful point rather than repeating the first note. Stop after that follow-up.

Stop immediately after a decline, opt-out, negative response, or request not to contact. Mark bounced addresses invalid; a verified fallback may then be used.

## Measure outcomes

Review after 40 initial emails or four weeks:

- eligible applications with a researched contact;
- verified-work-email rate;
- reply and conversation rates;
- interview conversion by role family, contact type, and message pattern;
- bounce, opt-out, and negative-response rates.

Improve recipient selection and message quality based on measured outcomes, not send volume.
